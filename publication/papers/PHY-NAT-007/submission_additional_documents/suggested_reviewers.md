# Suggested Reviewers

## Primary Recommendations

### 1. Prof. Carlo Rovelli
**Institution**: Center for Theoretical Physics, Aix-Marseille University, France  
**Email**: carlo.rovelli@cpt.univ-mrs.fr  
**Research Focus**: Loop quantum gravity, relational quantum mechanics, foundations of quantum theory  
**Relevance to Our Work**: Prof. Rovelli's relational interpretation of quantum mechanics aligns with our information-centric approach to quantum gravity. His work on the absence of fundamental time and the emergence of thermal time provides conceptual parallels to our collapse-originated framework.  
**Recent Publication**: "Helgoland: Making Sense of the Quantum Revolution" (2021)

### 2. Prof. Abhay Ashtekar
**Institution**: Institute for Gravitation and the Cosmos, Pennsylvania State University, USA  
**Email**: ashtekar@gravity.psu.edu  
**Research Focus**: Loop quantum gravity, quantum geometry, quantum cosmology  
**Relevance to Our Work**: Prof. Ashtekar's foundational work on loop quantum gravity and quantum geometrodynamics offers complementary perspectives to our prime-indexed collapse approach. His expertise in quantum cosmology would provide valuable insights on the cosmological implications of our framework.  
**Recent Publication**: "Quantum Geometry and Gravity: Theory and Phenomenology" (Annual Review of Nuclear and Particle Science, 2021)

### 3. Prof. Juan Maldacena
**Institution**: Institute for Advanced Study, Princeton, USA  
**Email**: malda@ias.edu  
**Research Focus**: AdS/CFT correspondence, quantum gravity, black hole information paradox  
**Relevance to Our Work**: Prof. Maldacena's work on holographic principles and information preservation in black holes is directly relevant to our predictions about black hole information interference patterns. His expertise in duality principles could provide critical evaluation of our unification approach.  
**Recent Publication**: "Wormholes and the black hole interior" (Journal of High Energy Physics, 2022)

### 4. Prof. Francesca Vidotto
**Institution**: Department of Physics and Astronomy, Western University, Canada  
**Email**: fvidotto@uwo.ca  
**Research Focus**: Quantum cosmology, Planck-scale physics, primordial universe  
**Relevance to Our Work**: Prof. Vidotto's expertise in quantum cosmology and Planck-scale physics makes her ideally suited to evaluate our model's implications for the early universe. Her work on spin networks provides interesting comparative perspectives to our prime-indexed attractor approach.  
**Recent Publication**: "Quantum Gravity and the Primordial Universe: A Conceptual Reassessment" (Foundations of Physics, 2023)

### 5. Prof. Gerard 't Hooft
**Institution**: Institute for Theoretical Physics, Utrecht University, Netherlands  
**Email**: g.thooft@uu.nl  
**Research Focus**: Quantum field theory, black hole information paradox, fundamental aspects of quantum mechanics  
**Relevance to Our Work**: As a Nobel laureate with pioneering work on the holographic principle and black hole information, Prof. 't Hooft can evaluate the fundamental viability of our approach. His recent work on cellular automata models of quantum mechanics has conceptual overlaps with our recursive field structure.  
**Recent Publication**: "The Cellular Automaton Interpretation of Quantum Mechanics" (Springer, 2016, with ongoing research)
